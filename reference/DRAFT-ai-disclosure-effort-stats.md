# DRAFT — measured effort, for the AI disclosure revision

Not published. Every figure below was computed from files on disk on 2026-08-17, and each
one says how it was measured so a reader can reproduce it. Where a number understates the
work, that is noted rather than rounded up.

| | |
|---|---|

Every one of those documents was found, requested, downloaded and filed by hand: city

property assessments, Secretary of State filings, statutes, audits, podcast audio.

Collection ran **June 23 to August 17, 2026** — 41 documents in June, 118 in July, 16 in
August.

Before any of that, there was an earlier version of this project: a general data-center
investigation with no Gallatin focus at all. Its files date to **June 4, 2026**, and by
June 12 it was a **19-section, 9,000-word document** covering the thesis, TVA, Meta, xAI,
Fisk, ratepayer impact, employment, Hendersonville, eminent domain, a ledger of claims,
and open threads. Quid-Pro-NO exists because one question inside that document would not
close.

## What the writing actually took

| | |
|---|---|
| Days worked at the terminal | **16** (June 30 – August 18) |
| Separate working sessions | **25** |
| Instructions typed | **880** |
| Words typed in those instructions | **47,344** |
| Average instruction length | **53 words** |

**These numbers are a floor, and a measurably incomplete one.** Three reasons:

1. **The record was being deleted as it was made.** Claude Code keeps session history for
   30 days by default, and that setting was never changed until August 17. The terminal
   record begins abruptly on June 30 because everything before it had already been erased.
   The project itself dates to June 4.
2. **They count one tool.** Earlier work was done in a browser. A single export of those
   conversations runs to **123,910 words**.
3. **They count typing.** Not reading 6,155 pages, not driving out to look at the
   buildings, not the weeks of getting nowhere before the first document was saved.

The honest span is **June 4 to August 18, 2026 — roughly eleven weeks** — of which the
terminal record covers the last seven.

## Why this is the opposite of "type a prompt, publish the output"

861 instructions averaging 54 words is not a prompt. It is a conversation, and most of it
is argument: rejecting a proposed approach, correcting a wrong reading of a document,
sending the machine back to look again.

A concrete example from the day these figures were computed. Asked to source a claim about
a TVA plant, the tool searched TVA's own website, found nothing, and reported the claim
unsupportable. TVA is a subject of this investigation. Its silence is not evidence. Sent
back to look for local reporting, it found a $1.1 billion overhaul documented by the
Chattanooga Times Free Press in 2013 and 2016. The claim was fine. The tool was wrong, and
the correction came from the human who knew the material.

The same day, three numbers in the draft were caught and fixed, all of them mine: a count
of Tennessee IDBs that was wrong, a "$0 to the county" line that was imprecise, and a
flagship claim about 501(c)(4) status that has no supporting document in the archive and
now carries a flag saying so.

## The standard

Every factual claim in the published work links to a document in this archive. The archive
is public. Read the filings yourself and check the arithmetic — the numbers above are only
context for how the pile was built. **The documents are the argument.**
