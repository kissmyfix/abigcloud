# Co-counsel review — `idb-functions-by-law-draft.md`

Reviewed against TCA §§ 7-53-301, -302, -305, -308 (Lexis prints in `tn_annotated_code/`), plus TCA 67-5-502(d), 67-5-605.
Date of review: 2026-07-25. **DRAFT — not for publication.**

Verdict: the *mechanism* is right, the *law* is loose. One claim is unsourced, one is backwards, and the largest problem is what the draft leaves out.

**Revision note (2026-07-25):** § 1 of this review originally challenged the draft's "$0" claim on leasehold-taxation grounds. That challenge was wrong and has been retracted after pulling the current text of TCA § 67-5-502(d). The draft was right. See § 1.

---

## What holds up

**Lease-leaseback mechanic (§1, steps 1-3).** Correct in substance. § 7-53-305(a)(1) declares the corporation "performing a public function in behalf of the municipality" and "a public instrumentality of such municipality," and exempts "the corporation and all properties at any time owned by it, and the income and revenues from the properties, and all bonds issued by it" from "all taxation in the state of Tennessee." Title in, exemption attaches, lease back out under § 7-53-302(a)(5).

**Section 115, not 501(c)(3) (§2).** Correct, and worth keeping — it is the single most common error in local coverage. § 7-53-308(a) supplies the state-law half: nonprofit corporation, no net earnings inure to any individual/firm/corporation, surplus goes to the municipality. Federal exemption flows from instrumentality status (IRC § 115 / § 103), not from charitable status.

**Comptroller reporting exists (§4).** § 7-53-305(e)(1) is real, and the draft correctly puts the filing duty on the *lessee*, not the board — annual report due to the Comptroller by Oct. 1, copy to the county assessor by Oct. 15, sixteen required data fields including PILOT amounts, the date property returns to the tax rolls, parcel ID, and city/county allocation.

## What is wrong

### 1. "The standard property tax bill instantly drops to $0." — CORRECT. I was wrong; retracting.

**Retraction.** An earlier version of this review argued the draft was incomplete because the private lessee's *leasehold interest* remains separately taxable under TCA § 67-5-502(d). I pulled the current text of § 67-5-502. That argument fails for the Gallatin deals. The draft is right and my objection is withdrawn.

**Where § 67-5-502 came into this, since it is a fair question.** Not from outside. Your own statute names it: § 7-53-305(e)(1)(H) requires the lessee's annual report to state "the amount of any property taxes paid on the leasehold assessment under § 67-5-502(d)." Chapter 53 points at it directly, so it is properly in scope. The question was never whether it applies to IDBs generally, but what it says now.

**What it actually says.** § 67-5-502(d) does begin by making separately-owned interests in real property assessable, "including the interest that the lessee may have in and to the improvements erected upon land where the fee, reversion, or remainder therein is exempt to the owner." That is the rule I was relying on. But the subsection ends with a carve-out added by **2019 Tenn. Acts, ch. 265, effective April 30, 2019**:

> "...unless the lessee's or a sublessee's interest in any such real property, including any improvements erected upon the land, is the subject of a lawful agreement between a lessee and this state or a local government, or instrumentality thereof, for payments in lieu of taxes, **entered into or amended on or after April 30, 2019**, in which case such property **shall be assessed solely to such governmental entity and shall be subject to all applicable exemptions**."

**In plain English.** Before April 30, 2019, a PILOT deal left a taxable leasehold sticking out that the assessor could reach. The 2019 amendment closed that. For any PILOT entered into or amended on or after that date, the whole thing is assessed only to the IDB, and the IDB's exemption applies to all of it. There is no second interest and no second bill. One parcel, one owner, one exemption, $0.

**Why this settles it for Gallatin.** The title transfer into the IDB was recorded in **August and October 2020**, well after the April 30, 2019 cutoff. So the 2019 carve-out governs, and the parcel record showing a $519,189,800 appraisal at a 0% ratio and a $0 assessment, with no separate leasehold record anywhere, is not an oversight by the assessor. **It is precisely what the statute directs.**

**What survives, and it is small.** § 7-53-305(e)(1)(H) is now largely a vestigial reporting field for post-2019 agreements. It still has force for PILOTs predating April 30, 2019 or never amended since. Noted only so the point is not re-litigated later; not a thread to pull now.

**Correction that carries forward.** Do not tell readers a taxable leasehold exists here. It does not. The stronger and simpler line is the one the draft already had: the legislature has closed the gaps around this exemption over time, most recently in 2019, and the result is that a half-billion-dollar facility is assessed at zero by operation of law.

### 2. "IDBs are heavily monitored for transparency by the state." — Backwards, and this is the story.
- The § 7-53-305(e) annual report is **self-reported by the lessee**, and the enforcement is a **$50 late fee**, rising to an additional **$500** only after written demand. That is not monitoring.
- The real compliance mechanism — § 7-53-305(f), biannual reports "detailing the lessee's compliance and noncompliance" and the "fiscal impact on revenues generated from ad valorem taxes" — **applies only to counties of 800,000+ population and municipalities within them.** Shelby and Davidson. **Sumner County is not covered.**

So Gallatin's IDB has no statutory ongoing compliance-reporting duty. The draft asserts the opposite. Fix this and the paragraph becomes the most valuable one in the document.

### 3. "~50% of normal tax rate," "30% to 50%." — No statutory basis. Cut or source.
Nothing in chapter 53 sets a percentage. PILOT amounts are negotiated. There are only floors and ceilings, and most are geographically gated:
- § 7-53-305(b)(1)(B): below-tax PILOTs capped at **20 years** plus a construction period not to exceed 3, unless **both** the ECD commissioner **and** the Comptroller make a written best-interest determination.
- § 7-53-305(b)(2): center-city/CBID floor.
- § 7-53-305(g), (h), (i): county-specific and no-property-tax-municipality constraints.

Presenting an unsourced percentage inside a diagram reads as a rule. It isn't one.

### 4. Constitutional framing (§1) is imprecise.
Art. II, § 28 is the authorization to tax and requires equal and uniform assessment; the constitutional exemption runs to the United States, the state, counties, cities and towns — **not** to a separately incorporated public corporation. The IDB's exemption is *statutory* (§ 7-53-305(a)(1)) and survives because Tennessee courts have treated the IDB as an **agent and instrumentality** of the creating municipality.

That agency theory *is* the load-bearing wall. The draft says "government-owned property is 100% tax-exempt" as if self-executing. It isn't. Rewrite this to name the agency doctrine — it is both more accurate and more useful, because an IDB behaving as a private deal-broker rather than a municipal agent is exactly where the theory strains.

## What is missing (and matters more than what's there)

### The five statutory preconditions to any PILOT — the compliance checklist
The draft has the board simply "negotiating" a PILOT. It cannot, unless:

1. **Formal delegation.** § 7-53-305(b)(1)(A)(i): authority to "negotiate, accept, or waive" PILOTs exists **only upon receipt of a formal delegation** from the municipality, granted **only upon a finding** that the payments or waiver further the corporation's public purposes.
2. **Notice to affected taxing jurisdictions.** § 7-53-305(b)(1)(A)(ii)-(iv): if the PILOT is below taxes otherwise payable, written notice to the chief executive of each affected jurisdiction; that CEO has **10 days** to demand legislative approval; silence lets the board proceed.
3. **Cost-benefit analysis.** § 7-53-305(b)(1)(C): the corporation "shall attach to each agreement an analysis of the costs and benefits," in the manner prescribed by the ECD commissioner. **Every agreement.**
4. **Term cap.** § 7-53-305(b)(1)(B): 20 years + 3, or dual state sign-off.
5. **Public meeting.** § 7-53-305(j): before approving any PILOT, a public meeting with **at least 5 days' notice** stating time, place, and purpose.

Every one of these produces a document. Every one is checkable against Gallatin's minutes and files. This is the reporting roadmap.

### Board composition and conflicts — § 7-53-301
- Minimum **7** directors, uncompensated (expenses only, unless local ordinance says otherwise).
- **No director may be an officer or employee of the municipality.**
- Elected by the municipal governing body; staggered terms, 6 years after the initial cycle; holdovers serve until a successor is elected.
- If a chamber of commerce, board of trade, or similar civic organization exists in the municipality, **directors must be chosen from its membership** unless the governing body judges no member both suitable and available.
- § 7-53-301(b): each director must complete a **conflict of interest statement** acknowledging receipt of § 12-4-101, must **refrain from voting** on matters of direct interest, and must **disclose** indirect interests before voting.

### Open meetings — § 7-53-302(c)
"Any meeting held by the board of directors for any purpose whatsoever shall be open to the public." No committee carve-out on the face of the statute.

### Limits on power — § 7-53-302(b)
The corporation **may not operate a financed project as a business**, and **may not pledge the general credit or taxing power of the municipality** except per § 7-53-306.

### Money flow, corrected
The draft's diagram is directionally fine but omits the enforcement and discretion layers:
- § 7-53-305(d)(1): PILOT and lease payments, up to what ad valorem taxes would have been, are a **first lien on the fee interest** from January 1, enforceable by the corporation in circuit or chancery court with **10% interest** and attorney's fees.
- § 7-53-305(d)(3): amounts exceeding what is needed for debt service, if not timely paid, "may be collected by or on behalf of the city or county in the same manner as delinquent property taxes."
- § 7-53-305(e)(1)(J): the city/county split is fixed by the **economic development agreement**, not by board discretion.
- § 7-53-308(a): surplus goes to the municipality **only after the board determines** sufficient provision has been made for expenses, bonds, and other obligations. The board decides when "enough" is enough. Worth pressing on.

## Two housekeeping problems

**Currency.** The § 7-53-305 print in `tn_annotated_code/305.pdf` is stamped *"Effective until July 1, 2026. See the version effective on July 1, 2026,"* and its history line carries **2026 ch. 987, §§ 2-4**. Today is July 25, 2026 — **the version in the file is superseded.** I could not retrieve the text of ch. 987. Pull the current version before quoting § 7-53-305 anywhere, and diff it. If ch. 987 touched the delegation, notice, or reporting provisions, several conclusions above move.

**Citations.** The bracketed numerals `[3, 4, 5]` point to nothing. Replace with pinpoint statutory cites. A summary of what the law requires is worthless to a reader who cannot check it.

**Population gates.** Several chapter 53 provisions are keyed to county population — § 7-53-302(d) (900,000+), § 7-53-305(f) (800,000+), § 7-53-305(g)(2) (897,400–897,500, i.e. Shelby), § 7-53-305(i)(3) (900,000+), § 7-53-308(b) (900,000+). **None apply to Sumner County.** Any version of this summary used for a Gallatin story needs an explicit "applies to Sumner?" column, or it invites an easy correction.

---

## Bottom line

Keep §§ 1-2. The "$0" claim stands as written and needs no change; only the constitutional framing in § 1 needs rewriting, around the agency doctrine. Rewrite § 3 around the five statutory preconditions instead of an invented percentage. Invert § 4 — the finding is that state oversight of a Sumner County IDB is thin by design, not heavy. Add § 7-53-301 board composition and conflict rules, which the draft omits entirely and which are the most checkable thing in the chapter.
