# Gibson County IDB (Tyson Farms) vs. Gallatin IDB — Comparison Findings (2026-07-02)

Sources: `2019thru2025-gibson-990.xml` (raw IRS e-file XML, FYs 2024 & 2023 shown in detail), `assessment-gibson-tyson.pdf` and `assessment-gibson-tyson2.pdf` (Gibson County parcel assessments, Tyson poultry processing plant, Humboldt, TN).

## What does NOT distinguish Gallatin from a clean comparison case

**Mailing address routing to tenant's out-of-state HQ.** Gibson County IDB's own parcel record reads "IDB Gibson County, TN C/O Tyson Farms, Inc, 2200 W Don Tyson Pkwy, Springdale AR 72762" — structurally identical to Beretta's Accokeek, MD routing and Woolhawk's Menlo Park routing. Same pattern, third county, third unrelated tenant. This is very likely standard Tennessee assessor/IDB administrative practice, not a Gallatin-specific irregularity. Downweight this finding's significance in the broader case.

**The quitclaim-warranty-quitclaim land acquisition sequence.** Tyson's parcel: quitclaim to the IDB 1/31/2014 (same year as Beretta's own quitclaim sequence), a $569,162 warranty deed sale 9/6/2018 ("multiple parcels" qualification, same code as Beretta's 2008 sales), another quitclaim 12/17/2018, then a small $14,019 boundary-correction warranty deed 2/22/2019 ("physical difference" qualification). Same shape as Beretta's 2014 sequence. Confirms this is the ordinary, statewide statutory mechanism (T.C.A. § 7-53-305) operating as designed, not something engineered specifically for Gallatin.

**No independent financial audit.** Gibson County's 990 answers "no" (`IndependentAuditFinclStmtInd` = false) to having audited financial statements, same as Gallatin. This may be normal for small Tennessee IDBs generally — don't lean on the audit-gap alone as a Gallatin-specific Rev. Proc. 95-48 failure; it likely fails this factor for most small IDBs statewide.

## What DOES still distinguish Gallatin — governance and reporting, not the underlying mechanism

- **Files every year, no gaps**, since at least 2012 (earliest year with API data) through 2024 — vs. Gallatin's total public silence 2013–2020.
- **Mission statement is identical, verbatim, year over year** — "promote industry in Gibson County" (2023) / "TO PROMOTE INDUSTRY WITH GIBSON COUNTY" (2024) — paired with an honest, accurate "no significant change" answer (`SignificantChangeInd` = false both years). Contrast with Gallatin's mission-statement drift between 2021 and 2022 filings paired with a false "no" answer on the same question.
- **Real Schedule O governance disclosure:** "Kingsley Brock, Executive Director, reviews the 990 before it is filed... he will sign the 8879 giving his authorization to e-file the tax return." Also discloses quarterly finance-review meetings ("the organization holds quarter end meetings to review financial statements"). Both `MinutesOfGoverningBodyInd` and `MinutesOfCommitteesInd` are true.
- **Itemized, specific expense categories** in Schedule O, all from the single FY ending 6/30/2025 ($453,617 revenue year): Budget Contribution $25,536, Surveying $19,500, Engineering $16,015, Tree Cutting $9,700, Mowing/Landscaping $8,842, Professional Fees $8,825, Insurance $5,269, Accounting $3,495, Legal $1,795, Occupancy $271, Taxes & Licenses $21, Bank Charges $30, Park Expense –$2,769 (credit) — real vendor-level detail, not opaque lump "consulting/management fee" payments to unnamed recipients. Note this itemization isn't present every year — the prior year (FY ending 6/30/2024, $43,535 revenue) didn't require a Schedule O attachment (`ScheduleORequiredInd` = false) and only reports a lump expense total.
- **Revenue this same year breaks down as:** investment income (interest/dividends) $50,662, net gain on securities sales $386,691 (sold $400,000 in securities, cost basis $13,309), and "Crop Income" $16,264. The large net-asset gain that year came from portfolio activity, not from any tenant/PILOT-related revenue — rents (real or personal property) are $0 in every line item, every year on record.
- **Books kept by a real, named, licensed CPA firm** — ATA Advisory LLC / ATA PLLC / ATA CPA's, Milan, TN, consistent across years, prepared by a licensed preparer with a PTIN (Jack M. Matthis). Contrast with Stark's "PStark Consulting," which returns no active Tennessee SOS registration.
- **10-member board, all independent**, named consistently across years (Tommy Litton, Butch Reed Jr, Jack Murphree, Bob McCurdy, WT Sims, Mark Hampton, Jeff Gamble, Bill Foster, Vance Coleman, Greg Pillow as President, Bill Joyner as Secretary) — same zero-compensation/zero-hours pattern as Gallatin's board, so this specific point doesn't distinguish them, but the board is stable and consistently disclosed year to year, unlike Gallatin's board composition changes.
- Revenue is small and mostly "miscellaneous"/investment income year to year, with occasional gains from asset sales (2021, 2022) and securities sales (2025's $386,691 net gain came from selling $400,000 of securities, not land — correcting the earlier assumption that all large gains were land sales). No rental income reported in any year 2012–2024.

## Bottom line for the case

The structural mechanism (quitclaim title transfer, tenant mailing address, lack of independent audit) is common across Tennessee IDBs and not unique evidence against Gallatin. What remains a real, standing distinction is Gallatin's governance and disclosure failure relative to at least this one comparable, sizable peer: a decade of total public silence, a false answer on a material-change question, and unnamed/undocumented payments where Gibson County shows a named CPA firm and itemized vendor detail for materially the same kind of activity.
