## Handling Instructions
The state-law framework, condensed (this is now the canonical summary; the statutory text itself is in `tn_annotated_code/`):

- **TN Constitution, Art. II, § 28** — prohibits direct property tax abatement. The IDB mechanism exists to route around this: the IDB takes title, government-owned property is exempt. The exemption stands only if the IDB actually qualifies as a government instrumentality.
- **T.C.A. Title 7, Ch. 53** (Industrial Development Corporations Act) — the governing IDB statute. § 7-53-305 is the property/lease authority Leon's report cites. Because the Gallatin IDB is chartered by the City only (not jointly with Sumner County), its power to set the county's PILOT share to $0 without county sign-off is the live legal question.
- **Statutory document checklist for a valid PILOT** (what should exist in the record): (1) city delegation resolution — the 2008 tiered-PILOT resolution is the likely candidate; (2) written notice to the Sumner County executive; (3) the county's response; (4) attached cost/benefit analysis; (5) ECD Commissioner + Comptroller best-interest determinations if the term exceeds 20 years (Woolhawk is reported as 5-year, so likely N/A); (6) 5-day public meeting notice and record; (7) for a city-only IDB: an interlocal agreement or county approval justifying a reduced county share.
- **T.C.A. Title 6, Ch. 58** (§ 6-58-114, JECD Boards) — separate advisory structure established by interlocal agreement, with statutory requirements for cross-sector membership, at-least-quarterly meetings, and a joint funding formula; per Isbell, the Sumner JECD Board lacks the required funding formula. The JECD Board doesn't execute PILOTs (the IDB does) — its relevance is as a parallel structure showing the county's economic-development governance fragmented across boards with inconsistently enforced statutory obligations.
