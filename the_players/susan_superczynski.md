## The omission

**Across eight organizations and every filing this project holds, the person named as
principal officer is disclosed in Part VII, the list of officers, directors and trustees,
in every case but four. All four are Children Are People, FYE2020 through FYE2022, and all
four name her.**

It is not the preparer's habit. **McMurray Fox & Associates** filed all four, and the same
firm listed Donnie Crenshaw correctly on CAP's own FYE2019 return, PJ Davis and Bethany
Sullivan correctly for Gallatin Shalom Zone FYE2018 to 2022, and Jimmy Johnston and Chris
Taylor correctly for Forward Sumner FYE2018 to 2024. The omission ends the moment CAP changes
preparers: **Dempsey Vantrease & Follis** takes over for FYE2023 and Brent Wood appears in
Part VII immediately.

A preparer files the roster the client supplies. Whatever the reason, the list that left
Children Are People three years running did not have her on it.

## Why she matters

- **She was not new in FY2023.** The FY2023 return introduces her as the organization's
  **first-ever paid executive director** at $53,432. She had by then been signing as its
  principal officer for three years. The board rosters name her for the first time in the
  year of the clean sweep, which makes her the second person the sweep left standing.
  See `memory/MEMORY.md` and the CAP handoff.
- **Her three undisclosed years bracket the money.** The **$1,200,000** lands in
  **FYE 2022-06-30**, visible only by arithmetic: every later return restates that year's
  contributions **$1,200,000 lower**, the signature of a Schedule A *unusual grant*
  exclusion. Schedule A instructions require an unusual grant to be described in Part VI.
  Both later returns' Part VI say only that the treasurer reviews the 990. **The $1.2M is
  described nowhere.** The November 2021 gala that produced the building falls inside the
  same fiscal year.
- **Part VII is where authority is established.** Unusual-grant treatment turns partly on
  whether the donor was a disinterested party and whether they stood in a position of
  authority over the organization. Part VII column (E) is also the only line reporting
  compensation from **related** organizations, and Schedule L's interested-person
  disclosures key off the officer list. A thin officer list is a thin Schedule L.
- **She is the contact on the building.** The Sumner County assessor still records parcel
  `125E-A-010.00`, **805 Teal Drive**, as **CHILDREN ARE PEOPLE C/O SUSAN SUPERCZYNSKI**,
  classed commercial, sale date **12 January 2026**, seventeen days before the dissolution
  filing, and roughly eighteen months after she left the organization.
- **The address moves with the money.** CAP files from **117 East Winchester, Gallatin**
  through FYE2022 and from **805 Teal Drive** from FYE2023 on. One FY2023 return, the one
  she signed as *treasurer* on 2024-05-15, still gives the old address; the other, signed by
  Brent Wood on 2024-09-10, gives the new one.
- **Signed as treasurer once.** On 2024-05-15 she signed a FYE2023 return as **TREASURER**,
  a title she holds on no roster. Brent Wood is the treasurer of record for that year.

## What this does not establish

Nothing here shows she made a decision about the roster, knew what Part VII required, or had
any relationship to whoever gave the $1,200,000. An unpaid executive director may not be an
officer under an organization's bylaws, and a preparer completes the form from the client's
own list, so this is a **documented omission, not a proven violation**. What makes it worth
carrying is the comparison set: forty-two filings in this archive disclose the principal
officer and four do not, and the four are consecutive, one organization, one person, ending
in the year the money arrived.

She may equally be the person who kept a small charity running for three unpaid years and
was finally put on salary for it. Both readings fit the filings as they stand.
