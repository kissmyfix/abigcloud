# James Fenton

Former Executive Director of the Gallatin Economic Development Agency (GEDA), ~12 years (started July 2012, per his own account; "mostly retired" as of the 2026 Growing Pains interview, "a couple of fingers in the pot still... working on some projects"). Before Gallatin: six years as economic development director in Cheatham County. The central operator of the Woolhawk/Meta recruitment.

## Why he matters

- Signed the 2017 NDA for Project Skillet (the deal that became Woolhawk/Meta).
- **July 19, 2018**: IDB registered-agent control moved from the City Attorney's office to GEDA — his shop — two months after Woolhawk negotiations began. Flagged in `memory/MEMORY.md` as the potential smoking gun: independent legal oversight removed at the moment it mattered most.
- On-record quote (Data Center Knowledge): Meta "will become the largest revenue source in the county."

## From the Growing Pains interview (Sumner County Republican Freedom Caucus podcast; transcript: `podcasts/transcripts/fenton-on-podcast-full.txt`)

- Project Skillet timeline in his words: 2016, "a company that TVA had brought to us"; went to Birmingham, AL first, returned two-three months later; 800+ acres assembled from private owners.
- The UT land chain, narrated as a war story: farm in a trust, amendment required 61 votes (24 county commissioners); fallback owner University of Tennessee "for agricultural experimentation"; UT president DiPietro initially routine, then reversed ("somebody has had their eyes on that for quite a while"); **Randall Boyd — former TN ECD commissioner — became UT president**, board of trustees newly shrunk, each interviewed beforehand by "one of our elected officials"; trustees signed off. Third fallback was a church, brought around with "some remuneration."
- A rival utility district's water fiefdom nearly killed the deal three weeks before announcement; resolved in a board meeting attended by the state senator, the ECD commissioner, and mayors.
- **"We didn't give them anything... They paid for everything"** — from the negotiator of a 20-year-per-building abatement (R2005-24). In the entire victory-lap episode he never says Woolhawk, PILOT, abatement, or IDB.
- Claims the 161kV line through the property made it power-ready — yet Meta later donated ~6 acres to TVA for a ~$9M substation built solely for Meta's load (rate-base funded; needs document sourcing).
- Rapid-fire doctrine: most underrated ingredient = "personal relationships"; biggest deal killer communities create = **"public discord"**; best reason to say no to a project = "wages"; praised Gallatin "thinking about growing up instead of growing out... redoing City Hall."
