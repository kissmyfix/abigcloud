# Susan High-McAuley

Gallatin native, cum laude UT College of Law grad (1993). Practiced at Bone McAllester Norton PLLC's Sumner County office (Hendersonville) before city government. Unanimously appointed Gallatin City Attorney by the City Council and Mayor Jo Ann Graves at a special-called meeting in June 2014, starting salary $120,000, effective June 30, 2014. Still serving as City Attorney as recently as September 2024. Currently also Middle Tennessee Director for the Tennessee Municipal Attorneys Association (2025–2026).

## Why she matters

She was the Gallatin IDB's **registered agent from October 2015 to July 2018** (per the SOS filing history, `sumner_county/gallatin_idb_data/1994-2026-idb-gallatin.pdf`) — meaning during that window, the IDB's institutional/legal home was literally the City Attorney's office, and she was that City Attorney. That control was transferred to GEDA on 7/19/2018, two months after Woolhawk negotiations began (per Fenton's 2017 NDA for Project Skillet and the May 2018 return as Project Woolhawk) — the move `memory/MEMORY.md` flags ("Fenton's July 2018 move as potential smoking gun") as removing independent legal oversight at the precise moment it would have mattered most.

Timeline overlap worth sitting with:
- **6/30/2014** — High-McAuley becomes City Attorney.
- **7/28/2014–8/28/2014** — the Beretta land quitclaim/warranty-deed/quitclaim sequence happens entirely on her watch, weeks into her tenure.
- **10/8/2015** — she becomes the IDB's registered agent directly.
- **8/2017** — Fenton signs the NDA for Project Skillet (later Huntsville).
- **5/2018** — Project Woolhawk begins.
- **7/19/2018** — IDB oversight moves from City Attorney (her office) to GEDA — two months into Woolhawk.
- She remains City Attorney continuously through at least 2024, meaning she was in city government, presumably reviewing or approving ordinances, through the entire Woolhawk PILOT negotiation, signing (2021), and the IDB's first-ever 990 filing.
